# ETH refutation claims await human verification

## Summary

P vs NP stands where it stood: the Clay Mathematics Institute lists it as unsolved ([CMI](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/)), no claimed proof from 2023–2026 is accepted, and nothing published in October bears on it directly. The October news is narrower. OpenAI (26 October 2026) and Anthropic (27 October UTC) each published a paper and a Lean 4 formalisation claiming a deterministic algorithm that counts the satisfying assignments of k-CNF formulas in 2^{o(n)} time, which would refute the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) ([OpenAI repository](https://github.com/openai/math); [Anthropic folder](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/eth)). Three questions must be kept apart. Does the Lean code compile on standard axioms? Yes: outside parties have recompiled both ([replications thread](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/30/lean-replications/)). Do the formal statements faithfully say that ETH is false? Volunteers' hand readings say so; no named complexity theorist has confirmed it. Is the written mathematics understood and accepted by the field? Not yet: the three designated examiners decline to certify it, and nobody has published an error ([examiners' letter](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/)). The fair reading is plausible but unverified. Even if correct, the result does not give P = NP, speeds up no SAT solver and breaks no deployed cryptography. Both labs have a commercial interest in such announcements. This report was prepared with an AI model made by Anthropic, one of the two labs concerned; the same scepticism has been applied to both.

## 1. What each lab claimed

Both labs claim the same kind of object: an exact counting algorithm for k-SAT whose exponent grows more slowly than any constant times n. Neither claims P = NP. Anthropic's paper says its result does not bear on P vs NP directly ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)), and OpenAI's says it does not give subexponential algorithms for NP problems in general ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). Both were released on GitHub; an arXiv title search for OpenAI's paper returned nothing on 11 November ([arXiv query](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=ti:%22Exponential+Time+Hypothesis+is+False%22&max_results=10)). Anthropic's paper is also deposited on Hexagon as version 1, dated 27 October and tagged as primarily AI-generated ([Hexagon record](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00577v1)).

Both theorems put roughly n/√log n in the exponent, so the claimed running times stay far above polynomial. OpenAI's bound appears in three forms that should not be merged: the abstract gives `2^{n/(log n)^{1/2+o(1)}}`, Theorem 1.1 gives the bounds in the table, and **the Lean development proves only the weaker exponent** with (log log n)², which matches the paper's Section 6.4 argument and not the Appendix D argument behind the headline ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf); [Lean note](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/378.md)). The weaker bound is still 2^{o(n)}, so the Lean statement as OpenAI describes it would still refute ETH. Anthropic's main body proves only that for every δ > 0 some algorithm counts 3-CNF solutions in O(2^{δn}) time; the full Theorem 1 bound is proved in appendices, and the bound in n draws on Appendix C, which is labelled a proof sketch ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). Wikipedia's ETH article, as retrieved on 11 November, reports a bound with a different denominator from either paper; this report follows the papers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exponential_time_hypothesis)).

The two formal developments state different things. Anthropic's statement file defines ETH as s_3 > 0 and states its negation as a theorem, alongside a main theorem asserting one machine and one constant for all k ([MainResults.lean](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/formal-math/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/MainResults.lean)). OpenAI's challenge file contains no definition named ETH: its formal claim is the existence of the counting machine, stated over its own CNF type, encoding and machine model in a 7.8 kB file that fronts about 710,000 lines of proof ([challenge file](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/ComparatorChallenges/SubexponentialSAT.lean); [solution directory](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/main/lean/OAI/Algorithms/SubexponentialSAT)). Anthropic is explicit about limits: the Lean was written by AI models and reviewed by other instances of the same models, no person was known to have checked it at the time of writing, no machine checks that the statement file says what the paper says, and the encoding and word-RAM simulation steps lie outside the Lean proof ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf); [README](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth)).

Discovery and publication dates must be kept apart. Each lab says its model found the result on **16 October 2026**; both dates are self-reported and no public record predates 26 October ([post](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/); [Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). OpenAI published on **26 October**; Anthropic's commit followed at **03:07 UTC on 27 October**, about five and a half hours after an OpenAI staff member's announcement post, which the notes read through a third-party mirror ([PR #3](https://github.com/openai/math/pull/3); [commit](https://api.github.com/repos/anthropics/formal-math/commits/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5); [mirror](https://api.fxtwitter.com/__alpoge__/status/2114838067953770981)). Anthropic's paper calls OpenAI's a similar result reached independently and concurrently and cites OpenAI's preprint URL, yet its PDF creation timestamp precedes OpenAI's commit; the notes leave this unresolved, and the metadata should not be used to argue priority ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)).

The labs' own framing needs weighing. OpenAI states the ETH result flatly as proved and Lean-verified, while describing the surrounding collection as raw research data in which unformalised results could have issues ([post](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/); [README](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/README.md)). That collection has already needed repair: on 7 October OpenAI withdrew three manuscripts over a sign error and revised fourteen others ([changelog](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md)), and Johan Commelin of the Mathlib Initiative told Quanta that some formalisations appear to prove weaker statements than their papers ([Quanta column](https://www.quantamagazine.org/updates/transformation/)). In October 2025 an OpenAI executive's post about ten Erdős problems was deleted once the solutions proved to be already in the literature ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/leading-openai-researcher-announced-a-gpt-5-math-breakthrough-that-never-happened/)). OpenAI's ETH paper also cites another unreviewed OpenAI preprint as the Unique Games Theorem ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). Anthropic's release was lower-key and its README calls the folder an unmaintained research artefact, but the headline is still an unqualified refutation, and the no-human-input statement comes without prompts, attempt counts or transcripts ([README](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth)). The commercial setting matters: Anthropic's news page lists an S-1 filing for a proposed initial public offering, dated 26 October 2026 ([news index](https://www.anthropic.com/news)), and OpenAI says it is working to release the model behind its results ([earlier post](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/)).

## 2. Independent scrutiny so far

Three labels are used. *Verified* means independently reproduced, or re-fetched from the primary page by this report's coordinator on 11 November 2026. *Plausible* means supported by named outside parties or uncontested independent work, but not checked end to end. *Only claimed* means resting on a lab's own statement or a single unreviewed source. Everything not marked as coordinator-checked rests on the researchers' notes.

The publication record is **verified**. The two repositories, their commit hashes, dates, authors and commit messages, the examiners' open letter and Anthropic's Hexagon deposit were re-fetched directly on 11 November ([Anthropic folder](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/eth); [OpenAI repository](https://github.com/openai/math); [examiners' letter](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/); [Hexagon record](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00577v1)).

Question one, whether the Lean compiles on standard axioms, is **verified**. Totó Busnello (31 October) built both developments: OpenAI's took 3 h 47 min over 12,014 build jobs, Anthropic's 42 min 28 s on five cores, and in both the main theorems depend only on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, with `sorry` appearing only in Anthropic's statement-only challenge file, as its README predicts. Carlos Toledo replayed both from pinned commits through Comparator, Lean's kernel, nanoda and leanchecker; Nathaniel Jose ran both check suites on macOS ([replications thread](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/30/lean-replications/); [Toledo's records](https://carlostoledo.co/eth/)). Two caveats apply. OpenAI's own configuration disables the second kernel, Jose's nanoda attempts on it did not complete, and one of Toledo's stricter checker runs on it did not finish. And these are volunteers reporting in blog comments, Toledo with Claude's assistance and Jose with OpenAI Codex.

Question two, whether the formal statements mean that ETH is false, is **plausible** (disputed: Alexander Arefiev, relaying Toledo, objects that the Lean counts Turing-machine steps and so does not check the word-RAM running time the papers claim; Busnello adds that OpenAI's unary-encoding cost is not established; Harvey Friedman objects in general to treating Lean as a gold standard). Busnello and Toledo read the statements by hand, and two readings found Anthropic's `ETHIsFalse` to be the Impagliazzo–Paturi condition s_3 = 0. Because OpenAI's Lean defines no ETH, Toledo wrote a textbook ETH in Lean and proved that each lab's statement refutes it, later porting both into one Lean version ending at ¬ETH on a standard multitape machine. He states the limits himself: bridge and checks share one author, were written with Claude, and whether a Lean ETH equals the textbook ETH remains a reading ([Toledo's records](https://carlostoledo.co/eth/); [replications thread](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/30/lean-replications/); [issue #36](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/issues/36); [Aaronson thread](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)). No review by a named complexity theorist or Mathlib maintainer was found, and neither Lean development has been checked line by line by a named human expert.

Question three, whether the written mathematics is correct as judged by complexity theorists, is **plausible** and not yet accepted. Russell Impagliazzo, Ramamohan Paturi and Ryan Williams, whom OpenAI approached, opened a community blog on 26 October stating: "Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim." No later post reports a verdict or an error ([examiners' letter](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/); [blog index](https://ethdiscussion.blog/)). Their independence is qualified: TechCrunch says OpenAI asked Williams to help interpret the proof, in which he found two or three parts alien ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/frontier-ai-labs-say-their-models-refuted-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-what-does-that-mean/)), while he and Paturi told AFP that the companies control what is published and when ([AFP](https://kuwaittimes.com/article/52682/world/shock-vindication-regret-scientists-react-as-ai-claims-to-overturn-their-lifes-work/amp)). Lance Fortnow says he has not verified the proof but treats ETH as finished ([Fortnow](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/10/the-exponential-time-refutation.html)); Scott Aaronson raised his personal probability of P = NP from 3% to 5% without claiming a check ([Aaronson thread](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)); Bill Gasarch adopts correctness as a working assumption ([Gasarch](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/11/3sum-and-apsp-and-eth-oh-my.html)). Re-expositions exist: Ryan O'Donnell's, produced with LLM assistance ([O'Donnell](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/some_eth_false_exposition.pdf)), and Shiva Kintali's, which proves only the one-algorithm-per-ε form ([Kintali](https://shivakintali.github.io/papers/ETH.pdf)); Jesper Nederlof finds Anthropic's approach intuitive and is writing it up, and Shachar Lovett has asked a structural question without alleging an error ([examiners' letter thread](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/)). Press coverage quotes almost only the three examiners, none of whom says the proof is correct ([NYT via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/11/09/how-ai-just-blew-up-a-key-tenet-of-computer-science)).

OpenAI's headline Theorem 1.1 bound is **only claimed**, resting on the unformalised Appendix D argument (disputed: OpenAI's post says the result was verified in Lean, but Toledo identifies the Lean statement with the paper's weaker Proposition 6.4, Jonathan Baxter made the same point on Aaronson's blog on 26 October, and OpenAI's own scope note concedes it) ([Lean note](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/378.md); [Toledo's records](https://carlostoledo.co/eth/); [Aaronson thread](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)).

Provenance is **only claimed**. The 16 October dates, the independence of the two efforts and Anthropic's no-human-input statement rest on the labs alone ([post](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/); [Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). ## 3. What refuting ETH would and would not mean

ETH, in Impagliazzo and Paturi's formulation, says s_3 > 0, where s_k is the infimum of the exponents δ for which k-SAT is solvable in O(2^{δn}) time ([Impagliazzo–Paturi](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturi_2001_jcss.pdf)). A refutation must solve 3-SAT in 2^{εn} time for every ε > 0. One 2^{o(n)} algorithm suffices; a better constant base does not, because ETH is exactly the claim that the best base exceeds 1. The best proven bounds are about 1.307^n randomised and 1.328^n deterministic ([Scheder](https://theoretics.episciences.org/13222); [Liu](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07901)). Both labs claim a single deterministic machine, the strongest form.

**ETH false implies SETH false.** SETH says s_k tends to 1 as k grows; if s_3 = 0, sparsification gives s_k = 0 for every k, so the limit is 0 ([Impagliazzo–Paturi](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturi_2001_jcss.pdf)). **SETH false does not imply ETH false**: the negation of SETH is one exponent below 1 that works for every k, which is still exponential time ([survey](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/surveyETH.pdf)). **ETH false does not imply that the Orthogonal Vectors conjecture is false**: SETH implies OV, the converse is not known, and the 3-SUM and APSP hypotheses are likewise not known to be affected ([Vassilevska Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/eccentri.pdf)). **A subexponential counting algorithm implies a subexponential decision algorithm**, since counting answers the decision question, so refuting #ETH refutes ETH but not the reverse ([Dell et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775)). Both papers also claim NSETH falls, and OpenAI's adds Gap-ETH; the literature notes did not confirm those steps from an explicit source, so they stand as the labs' claims ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf); [Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)).

**P = NP implies ETH false**, because polynomial time is 2^{O(log n)}, well inside 2^{o(n)}; equivalently, **ETH implies P ≠ NP** ([Aaronson survey](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pnp.pdf)). **ETH false does not imply P = NP**: 2^{o(n)} includes running times such as 2^{√n} that are nowhere near polynomial, and NP-complete planar problems are already solvable in 2^{O(√n)} time without consequence for P vs NP ([survey](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/surveyETH.pdf)). Williams had set separate personal likelihoods on the three statements beforehand: P ≠ NP 80%, ETH 70%, SETH 25% ([Williams](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/likelihoods.pdf)).

**ETH false does not imply subexponential algorithms for every NP problem**: polynomial-time reductions enlarge instances, which erases a gain this small, as OpenAI's paper, Fortnow and Aaronson all say ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf); [Fortnow](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/10/the-exponential-time-refutation.html); [Aaronson thread](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)). **ETH false implies subexponential algorithms for the problems equivalent to 3-SAT under subexponential-time reductions**: k-SAT for every k, k-colourability, k-set cover, independent set, clique, vertex cover, and satisfiability of linear-size circuits measured by circuit size ([Impagliazzo–Paturi–Zane](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~paturi/myPapers/pubs/ImpagliazzoPaturiZane_2001_jcss.pdf)). For Dominating Set and Hamiltonian Path the classical literature in the notes proves only the opposite direction, that a 2^{o(n)} algorithm would refute ETH ([survey](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/surveyETH.pdf)). Anthropic's Corollary 7, which lists Dominating Set, and OpenAI's Corollary 1.3 on counting independent sets, colourings and perfect matchings are therefore the labs' claims, not established consequences ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf); [OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). **ETH false implies FPT = M[1] but does not imply FPT = W[1]**, because M[1] sits inside W[1] ([Downey–Thilikos](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3161)). **ETH false implies that some problem in E^NP has no linear-size circuits** ([Jahanjou–Miles–Viola](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.3171)); OpenAI's sharper circuit bound is its own claim. **ETH false does not imply that ETH-conditional lower bounds reverse**: a theorem saying no faster algorithm exists unless ETH fails becomes vacuous, not an algorithm ([survey](https://sites.cs.ucsb.edu/~daniello/papers/surveyETH.pdf)).

**ETH false does not imply faster SAT solving in practice.** Worst-case asymptotics and solver performance are separate matters: CDCL solvers handle industrial instances with millions of variables while provably needing exponential time on some small families, because their running time is bounded below by resolution proof length ([Buss–Nordström](https://jakobnordstrom.se/docs/publications/ProofComplexityChapter.pdf)). Anthropic says its bound beats exhaustive search only for n above about 10^{10,000} when m = 5n ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)), and Fortnow expects no variant ever to beat CDCL ([Fortnow, 4 November](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/11/another-step-towards-optiland.html)).

**ETH false does not imply that any deployed cryptosystem is broken.** No deployed primitive has a security proof resting on ETH or SETH. Factoring already has subexponential algorithms and lies in NP ∩ coNP ([Aaronson survey](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pnp.pdf)); lattice schemes rely on polynomial approximation factors for which no NP-hardness is known ([Peikert](https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~cpeikert/pubs/lattice-survey.pdf)); AES is judged by concrete cryptanalysis, where the best published key recovery on 128-bit keys costs about 2^{126.1} ([Bogdanov et al.](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/449)). A generic SAT algorithm is subexponential in circuit size, not key length: an AES-128 circuit has 36,663 gates, so the exponent per variable would have to fall below about 0.0035 to beat 2^128 search, against about 0.386 for today's proven 3-SAT algorithms. The notes assemble that arithmetic themselves; no published analysis was found ([circuit sizes](https://nigelsmart.github.io/MPC-Circuits/)). What is touched is theoretical: ETH-conditional hardness results for exact lattice problems lose their basis without yielding an algorithm ([Simons blog](https://blog.simons.berkeley.edu/?p=164)), and exponentially hard local one-way functions would be ruled out through Applebaum's chain, which leans on Gap-ETH implying ETH ([Applebaum](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2017/063/)). The one reassurance attributed to OpenAI, that it had checked and expected no cryptographic breaks, reaches the record only through Williams as relayed by TechCrunch ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/frontier-ai-labs-say-their-models-refuted-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-what-does-that-mean/)).

## 4. The state of P vs NP itself

Nothing about P vs NP itself changed in 2026. CMI still lists the problem as unsolved ([CMI](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/p-vs-np/)). Its 2018 rules require publication in a refereed qualifying outlet, at least two years of scrutiny and general acceptance, with either direction eligible; they do not mention AI or machine-checked proofs, and a paper on a closely related question does not qualify ([rules](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/millennium_prize_rules_0.pdf)). Gasarch's latest poll, run in 2018, had **88% of 124 respondents** expecting P ≠ NP ([poll](https://www.cs.umd.edu/users/gasarch/papers/poll3.pdf)). In June 2026 Fortnow wrote that the field lacks even a viable approach ([Fortnow, June](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/06/respect-p-v-np-problem.html)). After the October announcements Williams told TechCrunch that computer scientists should put a little more weight on P = NP ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/27/frontier-ai-labs-say-their-models-refuted-the-exponential-time-hypothesis-what-does-that-mean/)).

Three barriers explain why. **Relativisation** (Baker, Gill and Solovay, 1975): oracles exist relative to which P = NP and others relative to which P ≠ NP, so no argument that survives the addition of an arbitrary oracle, which covers plain simulation and diagonalisation, can settle the question ([BGS](https://doi.org/10.1137/0204037)). **Natural proofs** (Razborov and Rudich, 1997): a lower-bound proof that is constructive and applies to a large fraction of functions would yield a distinguisher against pseudorandom functions, so if such functions exist in a circuit class, natural proofs cannot give strong lower bounds against it; this barrier is conditional on that cryptographic assumption ([Razborov–Rudich](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.1997.1494)). **Algebrisation** (Aaronson and Wigderson, 2009): arithmetisation arguments of the IP = PSPACE kind, which escape relativisation, still cannot separate P from NP ([Aaronson–Wigderson](https://doi.org/10.1145/1490270.1490272)). None shows the problem to be unprovable; each marks the limit of a family of techniques, and few results evade all three at once ([Aaronson survey](https://www.scottaaronson.com/papers/pnp.pdf)). A later addition, the locality barrier, explains why hardness magnification has not delivered separations ([locality](https://doi.org/10.1145/3538391)).

The live programmes keep producing theorems without moving the frontier that matters. The best explicit lower bound for general Boolean circuits is still **3.1n − o(n)** ([Li–Yang](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/023/)). Williams's algorithms-to-lower-bounds programme gave NEXP ⊄ ACC0 and later NQP ⊄ ACC0 ([Williams](https://doi.org/10.1145/2559903); [Murray–Williams](https://doi.org/10.1145/3188745.3188910)); its range-avoidance offshoot now proves near-maximal 2^n/n circuit bounds, but for classes far above NP, most recently in a FOCS 2026 paper by Ren and Williams ([Chen–Hirahara–Ren](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12912); [Ren–Williams](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.09963)). Geometric complexity theory's clearest settled structural result is negative: occurrence obstructions cannot work ([Bürgisser–Ikenmeyer–Panova](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06431)). Meta-complexity has tied one-way functions to the average-case hardness of time-bounded Kolmogorov complexity and proved NP-hardness of the Minimum Circuit Size Problem only for variants or under extra assumptions ([Liu–Pass](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11514); [Hirahara](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2022/119/); [Goldberg et al.](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2026/091)). Unprovability work shows that some circuit lower bounds cannot be proved in weak arithmetic theories, which says nothing about ZFC ([Chen–Li–Oliveira](https://lmcs.episciences.org/16491)). The largest neighbouring advance, Williams's 2025 simulation of time t in space about √(t log t), concerns time against space ([Williams 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.17779)). No FOCS 2026 paper claims progress on P vs NP itself ([FOCS 2026](https://focs.computer.org/2026/accepted-papers/)).

No claimed resolution from 2023–2026 is accepted.

The Xu–Zhou case shows that journal publication alone settles nothing: Allender reports that the editorial process broke down and that the editor declined to retract ([Allender](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2025/08/some-thoughts-on-journals-refereeing.html)). The two Lean entries carry the lesson most relevant here: a compiling Lean development certifies a formal statement, and both of those are reported to rest on declared axioms, with no expert audit of what the statements mean. The ETH developments differ in resting on the three standard axioms only, which is why their open question is statement meaning and not hidden assumptions. Woeginger's list of claimed proofs stopped at 116 entries in 2016 and has no authoritative successor ([Woeginger](https://wscor.win.tue.nl/woeginger/P-versus-NP.htm)). Verified AI contributions to complexity theory remain modest: a refereed quantum-complexity paper in which, per secondary coverage, a model suggested a key function ([Aaronson–Harris–Witteveen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21131)) and computer-checked gadgets in a preprint ([Nagda et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18057)). STOC 2027 now requires disclosure of material LLM use and admits only human authors ([STOC 2027 call](https://acm-stoc.org/stoc2027/stoc2027-cfp.html)).
