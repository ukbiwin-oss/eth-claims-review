# ETH claims review

Two cited literature reviews of the claims, published on 26 and 27 October 2026, that models from OpenAI and Anthropic found algorithms refuting the Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH). The reviews are as of 11 November 2026.

- [eth-claims-and-quantum-advantage.md](eth-claims-and-quantum-advantage.md): if the claims are correct, which results in physics lose their premise and which are untouched. It covers classical simulation of quantum circuits, quantum-advantage arguments and their qubit estimates, boson sampling, and partition functions and tensor networks.
- [p-vs-np-and-eth-claims.md](p-vs-np-and-eth-claims.md): exactly what each lab claimed, the independent scrutiny so far, what refuting ETH would and would not mean, and where P vs NP stands.

The same reports are on the web at <https://www.lee-miller.com/eth>.

## What this is and is not

- These reviews report and cite. They do not prove anything new, and they do not check the labs' proofs.
- Statements marked "inference" are the authors' own reasoning. No expert has reviewed them.
- Every factual statement links to its source. Where a source could not be read, the report says so.

## How it was made

Prepared by Lee Miller with Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic. Anthropic is one of the two labs whose claims are reviewed, and the same scepticism is applied to both. The research and drafting were done by AI agents. Their cited links were checked automatically on 11 November 2026: in the physics report 44 of 46 load, and in the P vs NP report 99 of 106 load. The failures are paywalls, rate limits or sites that refuse automated requests, and each report says which ones it could not read.
