# ETH refutation empties lower bounds, spares supremacy

> **Correction, 12 November 2026.** The first version of this report (11 November) said that no concrete qubit or photon estimate changes, because the published counts rest only on conjectures that the claims do not contradict. That missed an extra assumption behind Dalzell et al.'s figures for 500-element circuits (208 IQP qubits, 420 QAOA qubits, 98 photons). The summary, row A12 of the table and sections 2, 4, 5 and 8 are corrected. The conjectures, and the 185/370/93 figures that rest on them alone, are as before.

## Summary

If the October 2026 claims are correct, the physics results that change are a specific set of worst-case, exact or near-exact lower bounds. They change by losing their premise, not by being shown false. **Every reduction in these papers runs from SAT or counting to the physics problem**: a fast simulator would give a fast SAT counter, not the reverse. A fast #k-SAT algorithm therefore makes those lower bounds vacuous; it is not a faster simulator. The bounds affected are the ETH- and SETH-based bounds on strong simulation (Huang–Newman–Szegedy in its fixed-k reading, Google's 2019 supplement, Morimae–Tamaki 2019) and the #ETH-based bounds for permanents, Tutte and Potts polynomials and exact tensor-network contraction. One conjecture, Morimae–Tamaki's Conjecture 7, appears to be refuted outright; that is the researchers' inference, not a published result. Untouched are the average-case, approximate assumptions behind random-circuit, IQP and boson-sampling advantage, and Dalzell et al.'s conjectures. On the published crossover estimates, no qubit or photon estimate changes at any size a machine can reach. Dalzell et al.'s headline figures for 500-element IQP and QAOA circuits (208 and 420 qubits) do rest on an extra assumption, that simulation cost grows linearly with the number of circuit elements, and the claims would break that assumption in the limit (section 4). The few algorithms actually claimed are narrow and unrefereed: sparse perfect matchings, chromatic polynomials, Boolean tensor contraction, and a blog-comment claim of exact sampling for linear-size circuits. No physicist has published on the question. This report was prepared with an AI model made by Anthropic, one of the two labs concerned.

## 1. The claims in one paragraph

OpenAI (26 October 2026) and Anthropic (27 October, UTC) each published a paper and a Lean 4 formalisation claiming a deterministic, worst-case algorithm that exactly counts the satisfying assignments of k-CNF formulas in time 2^{O(n log log n/√log n)}, refuting ETH, SETH and #ETH; Anthropic says it does not refute the Orthogonal Vectors conjecture ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf); [Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). Volunteers have recompiled both Lean developments on standard axioms ([replications thread](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/30/lean-replications/)). The three examiners OpenAI approached say their involvement is not certification, and nobody has published an error ([examiners' letter](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/)). [The companion report](p-vs-np-and-eth-claims.md) rates the claims plausible but unverified. The gains are asymptotic only. Majdoddin's unrefereed calculation puts the crossover for Anthropic's method at about 2^(8.6·10^9) variables for general 3-SAT, or about 2^420 clauses at clause density one ([arXiv:2610.41622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41622)). Dias and Lima find that OpenAI's certified bound beats 2^n at bounded density only when log₂(n+m) exceeds 10^9 ([arXiv:2611.00517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00517)).

## 2. How hardness assumptions enter physics

Everything below is conditional on the claims holding. Results that a source proves, results the labs or commenters claim, and the researchers' own inferences are kept apart, and inferences are marked "(inference: …)". The researchers found no published source, as of 11 November 2026, that sets out this mapping across these areas.

Hardness assumptions reach physics by four routes, and only the first is exposed to the claims.

The first route is **fine-grained conditional lower bounds**. A paper reduces k-SAT, #3-SAT or a gap version to a physics problem: computing an amplitude, a permanent, a Tutte polynomial or a tensor-network contraction. It then concludes that the physics problem needs 2^{Ω(n)} or 2^{n−o(n)} time unless ETH, SETH or #ETH fails. If the claims hold, those premises are false, and the theorem is still true but tells us nothing. OpenAI's paper states the same logic from the algorithm side: a faster algorithm for some target problem needs "a reduction in the other direction", from that problem to fixed-width SAT, with the number of variables kept under control ([OpenAI paper, p.5](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). It adds that 2^{o(n)} running times do not survive general polynomial-time reductions ([OpenAI paper, §1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)).

The second route is **polynomial-time complexity**: non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy, #P-hardness, and average-case conjectures about approximating output probabilities. Hangleiter and Eisert's review notes that these arguments "do not—and cannot" give quantitative runtime lower bounds, and that fine-grained results instead build on versions of SETH ([arXiv:2206.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04079)). A 2^{o(n)} algorithm for a SAT variant does not touch polynomial-versus-superpolynomial statements (inference: it puts no #P-hard problem in polynomial time).

The third route is **bespoke fine-grained conjectures** that are not CNF statements. Examples are Dalzell, Harrow, Koh and La Placa's poly3-NSETH and per-int-NSETH, which give the published qubit and photon counts ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). The fourth route is **algorithmic cost estimates**, such as tensor contraction costing time exponential in treewidth ([Boixo et al., arXiv:1608.00263](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00263)). The claims could reach the third and fourth routes only through a reverse reduction with linear blow-up. None is known that covers the dense instances within these conjectures' range. For circuits with only O(n) elements one exists, and section 4 sets out its consequence.

The pattern in the table follows from this (inference, drawn from the researchers' consolidated reading). What falls are results whose stated hypothesis is ETH, a fixed-k form of SETH, #ETH, or a gap or counting conjecture restricted to 3-CNF. Conjectures about degree-3 polynomials, dense permanents, general circuits or average-case approximation are not reached by any cited reduction. The table is the reference for sections 3 to 6.

## 3. Quantum circuit simulation lower bounds

**Huang, Newman and Szegedy (2018)** prove an unconditional bound, (n−2)(2^{n−3}−1), for monotone simulators (row A1). Their Theorem 3 says that, "Assume the SETH holds", any universal simulator computing amplitudes of poly(n)-gate circuits to precision 2^{−n}/2 needs 2^{n−o(n)} time ([arXiv:1804.10368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10368)). The reduction compiles a SAT formula into a reversible circuit whose all-zeros amplitude equals the fraction of satisfying assignments. A fast simulator would then decide SAT. The paper's SETH is posed for general CNF-SAT with poly(n) clauses and no width bound, and the authors state ETH separately as a 3-SAT statement ([arXiv:1804.10368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10368)). The fixed-k form of SETH implies this CNF-SAT form, but refuting the stronger statement does not refute the weaker one. The claimed algorithms are stated for fixed k (inference: so Theorem 3 as literally written is **unclear**, row A2, and depends on how the claimed running time grows with k; see section 8). The authors restrict themselves to worst-case instances. They leave average-case hardness and the typical precision 2^{−n/2} open, and they give no bound for sampling ([arXiv:1804.10368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10368)).

There is a discrepancy between a review's description and this paper's theorem. Hangleiter and Eisert describe the HNS 2^{n−o(n)} bound as resting on the exponential-time hypothesis ([arXiv:2206.04079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04079)), while the paper's Theorem 3 states SETH as its assumption ([arXiv:1804.10368](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10368)).

**Google's 2019 supremacy supplement** uses the fixed-k reading. It says that under SETH some n-qubit circuits need 2^{(1−o(1))n} time to estimate |⟨0|U|0⟩|² to additive error 2^{−(n+1)}. Its footnote 106 specifies circuits of depth n^{3k/2+5/2} and a SETH form in which k-SAT needs 2^{(1−2/k)n}. The supplement also calls the analogous bound for random circuits "an open question" ([arXiv:1910.11333](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11333)). A 2^{o(n)} algorithm eventually falls below 2^{(1−2/k)n} for every fixed k ≥ 3 (inference: so the corollary's premise is refuted, row A3). The researchers also note a caveat that predates October 2026. The corollary combines an AM protocol with SETH, so read literally it needs SETH to hold against AM-type algorithms. Morimae and Tamaki cite Williams's "Strong ETH breaks with Merlin and Arthur" as an MA refutation of SETH ([arXiv:1912.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06336)). The researchers did not check this beyond the citation.

**Huang, Newman and Szegedy's T-count paper** has two theorems that fare differently. Theorem 1 assumes ETH and says that deciding ⟨0|C|0⟩ ≠ 0 for a Clifford+T circuit with N T-gates takes 2^{εN} ([arXiv:1902.04764](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04764)). Because counting decides, a 2^{o(n)} counter refutes ETH, so this premise falls (row A4). The widely quoted constant 2^{2.2451×10^{−8}N} belongs to Theorem 2. That theorem is not a lower bound: it is an unconditional implication that a simulator this fast "would improve on" the best 3-SAT solver by reaching O(1.3^n) ([arXiv:1902.04764](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04764)). It remains true (row A5). Its 1.3^n benchmark would be beaten only at galactic sizes (inference). **Morimae and Tamaki's Theorem 6** is the same kind of ETH-conditioned strong-simulation bound for Clifford+T circuits, so its premise falls too (row A6) ([arXiv:1901.01637](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01637)).

Two claimed algorithms bear on this area. The first is in a blog comment of 3 November. Alexandru Gheorghiu (IBM) and Aparna Gupte (MIT), assisted by ChatGPT, claim classical *exact* sampling from quantum circuits built on the #SAT algorithm. Their first result covers n-qubit circuits with O(n) gates from a finite gate set, sampled in poly(n)·exp(n/√log n). Their second covers circuits with O(n) Hadamards and O(n·exp(o(√log n))) diagonal gates from {Z, CZ, CCZ}, sampled in exp(o(n)) ([ethdiscussion.blog, 3 November](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/11/01/announce-your-results/)). This is **claimed and unrefereed**. The note sits in a Google Drive folder that the researchers did not read, and the comment says nothing about supremacy. The comment's introduction calls the circuits "quasilinear-size", while the stated results are for O(n) gates. On its face the first result is what one would expect (inference: an exact output probability of an O(n)-gate circuit is a #SAT-type sum over O(n) path variables, and marginals then give samples).

The second claimed algorithm is OpenAI's Theorem D.1, which claims to contract Boolean tensor networks with s binary bonds in 2^{O(s/√log s)} ([OpenAI paper, p.53](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). Read literally, it would give exact amplitudes for Hadamard–Toffoli circuits with g gates in 2^{O(g/√log g)} (inference: that is subexponential in the number of qubits only when g = O(n); for poly-size circuits with g much larger than n it is worse than Schrödinger-style 2^n simulation, and the entry ring of modular integers does not cover T-gate phases). So the claims give no 2^{o(n)} simulator for general poly-size circuits. Markov and Shi's algorithm, exponential in treewidth, is unaffected (row C4) ([arXiv:quant-ph/0511069](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0511069)).

## 4. Quantum-advantage arguments and qubit estimates

**No estimate changes at any size a machine can reach, but one set of headline figures would lose an assumption.** The conjectures behind the published counts are not contradicted by the claims. Dalzell et al.'s figures for 500-element circuits rest on a further assumption about the simulator, and for IQP and QAOA circuits the claims would break it in the limit (below). Even where a premise falls, the claimed crossover lies near 2^(8.6·10^9) variables or beyond ([arXiv:2610.41622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41622)), against instances of 10^2 to 10^3 qubits.

The principal source of numbers is **Dalzell, Harrow, Koh and La Placa**. Their poly3-NSETH(a) asserts that any non-deterministic Word-RAM algorithm deciding whether a degree-3 polynomial over F2 is unbalanced needs 2^{an−1} steps in the worst case. Per-int-NSETH(b) asserts the same for deciding whether an integer permanent is non-zero. Poly3-ave-SBSETH(a′) is an average-case version that supports additive-error bounds ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). The authors take a = 1/2, b = 0.999 and a′ = 1/2. From the conjectures alone, with a budget of one century at 10^18 floating-point operations per second, they obtain 185 IQP qubits, 370 QAOA qubits and 93 photons. The bound then applies to circuits with about 1,060,000 gates, 2,110,000 constraints and 17,400 optical elements. Assuming in addition that simulation time grows linearly with the number of circuit elements, they conclude that IQP circuits with 208 qubits and 500 gates, QAOA circuits with 420 qubits and 500 constraints, and boson sampling with 98 photons and 500 optical elements are intractable on the same budget ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). They add that a constant-factor failure of the conjectures would raise these counts only logarithmically. Known algorithms already rule out poly3-NSETH(a) for a > 0.9965 ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)).

The paper's only link to ETH is its Theorem 2: assuming ETH, some constant a exists for which deterministic algorithms for poly3-NONBALANCED need Ω(2^{an}). The proof reduces sparsified k-SAT to a degree-3 polynomial with O(n+m) variables. A variant under #ETH covers counting zeros ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). The authors write that they "are unable to show" poly3-NSETH follows from any earlier conjecture ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). Theorem 2 becomes vacuous (row A11). The conjectures stand on their own (row A12). (Inference: the converse encoding is Tseitin-style, with one auxiliary variable per monomial plus an XOR chain, so it has about n + O(g) variables. DHKL's dense IQP instances have g ≈ n³/6 monomials, which makes a 2^{o(N)} bound in N = Θ(n³) useless against 2^{an}. Sparse polynomials with g = O(n) would plausibly become subexponential to count, but poly3-NSETH ranges over all degree-3 polynomials, so this does not refute it. The 185/370/93 figures need only the conjectures and stand with them.)

The 208/420/98 figures are a different matter. They are for circuits with 500 elements, which is the sparse case. The authors obtain them from the dense bound by a further assumption, that the runtime of the simulation algorithm grows linearly with the number of circuit elements, and they state the result for an algorithm "of this type" ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). (Inference: a 500-gate IQP circuit on 208 qubits corresponds to a degree-3 polynomial with 208 variables and at most 500 monomials. That polynomial encodes into a 5-CNF with about 700 variables whose satisfying assignments match the polynomial's zeros one to one. Output probabilities and marginals are sums of the same kind, so a 2^{o(N)} counter would give exact samples from circuits with O(n) gates in 2^{o(n)} time. Such a simulator's cost is not linear in the gate count. If the claims hold, the linear-scaling assumption therefore fails in the limit for circuits whose gate count grows in proportion to their qubit count, and the 500-element figures no longer bound every simulator, row A12b. On the crossover estimates above, the claimed algorithms are far slower than brute force at about 700 variables, so nothing changes at these sizes. The same route should apply to QAOA circuits with O(n) constraints, whose amplitudes are sums of roots of unity over local terms; this was not worked through. No such route was found for boson sampling; see section 5.) Gheorghiu and Gupte's second claimed result is stated for this regime: O(n) Hadamards and a nearly linear number of Z, CZ and CCZ gates (inference: it does not reach DHKL's dense instances, which have roughly n³/6 gates, but in the limit it would cover circuits of the 500-gate kind). Quantum Zeitgeist's unsigned analysis says the claims do "not obviously break" the DHKL assumption, but that this needs settling quickly ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.substack.com/p/the-openai-exponential-time-hypothesis)).

**Morimae and Tamaki (2019)** contains the one conjecture that appears to fall outright. Conjecture 7 says that any non-deterministic algorithm deciding whether gap(f) ≠ 0 for a 3-CNF with m clauses needs 2^{Ω(m)}, and it underlies Theorem 7, a multiplicative-error sampling bound for Clifford+T circuits ([arXiv:1901.01637](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01637)). (Inference: an exact deterministic #3-SAT count gives gap(f) = 2^n − 2·#f; unused variables can be discarded so that n ≤ 3m; a 2^{o(n)}·poly(m) running time is then 2^{o(m)}, and a deterministic algorithm is a special case of a non-deterministic one. Conjecture 7 is therefore **refuted**, row A7, and Theorem 7 loses its premise, row A8.) No source states this. Conjectures 4–6 concern general, log-depth and reversible circuits. A fast algorithm for the fixed-k CNF subclass does not reach them, so the DQC1 and HC1Q results built on them stand (row A9) ([arXiv:1901.01637](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01637)).

**Morimae and Tamaki (2020)** use average-case GapP conjectures that the authors call "different from ETH and SETH" ([arXiv:1912.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06336)). Two of the three supporting reasons they give would become false: that #ETH "has not been refuted for MA … and AM", and that SETH has not been refuted even on average ([arXiv:1912.06336](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.06336)). (Inference: a deterministic algorithm is in particular an MA algorithm, and a worst-case refutation is also an average-case one.) The conjectures themselves stand (row A10). **Aaronson and Chen's** HOG hardness rests on QUATH, an average-case, polynomial-time assumption; their arXiv text never mentions ETH or SETH ([arXiv:1612.05903](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.05903)). **Hayakawa, Morimae and Tamaki** use OV, 3-SUM and APSP ([arXiv:1902.08382](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.08382)), and Anthropic does not claim OV falls ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)).

Experimental supremacy proposals rest on algorithmic cost estimates rather than ETH. Boixo et al. note that tensor-contraction simulation of random circuits costs time exponential in treewidth, and they rely on average-case sampling arguments ([arXiv:1608.00263](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.00263)). The status of **QSETH**, the quantum analogue, is open (row A15). Quantum Zeitgeist asserts that fixed-width QSETH variants (Buhrman, Patro and Speelman, [arXiv:1911.05686](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.05686)) fall with the classical hypothesis ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.substack.com/p/the-openai-exponential-time-hypothesis)). Deshpande and Mishra posted a QSETH-based hardness paper on 10 November whose abstract does not mention the claims ([arXiv:2611.14411](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14411)).

## 5. Boson sampling and permanents

The theory behind photonic advantage sits almost entirely in the second route. Aaronson and Arkhipov prove that efficient exact classical BosonSampling would collapse the polynomial hierarchy (row B1). Their approximate result depends on conjectures about permanents of i.i.d. Gaussian matrices: the Permanent-of-Gaussians conjecture, which says multiplicative estimation is #P-hard, and permanent anti-concentration (row B2) ([arXiv:1011.3245](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3245)). Gaussian boson sampling uses hafnian analogues ([Hamilton et al., arXiv:1612.01199](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01199)), together with a hiding conjecture and partial average-case results ([Deshpande et al., arXiv:2102.12474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12474)). A July 2026 paper proves only a weak anti-concentration bound ([Meng et al., arXiv:2607.22088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22088)). None of these conjectures mentions ETH, SETH or #ETH (inference: they are polynomial-time, average-case statements, and a worst-case 2^{o(n)} exact counter does not put #P inside BPP^NP).

The fine-grained permanent bounds do fall. Dell, Husfeldt, Marx, Taslaman and Wahlén reduce #3-SAT to the permanent of a {−1,0,1} matrix with O(m) non-zero entries. They prove that no exp(o(m)) algorithm exists under #ETH, and for 0/1 matrices under rETH ([arXiv:1206.1775](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775)). Both premises fall, since OpenAI also claims to refute the randomised variants ([OpenAI paper, §1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). That gives row B4. Curticapean's #ETH bound for perfect matchings in sparse bipartite graphs falls likewise (row B5) ([arXiv:1511.02910](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02910)).

Here the labs do claim an algorithm. **OpenAI's Corollary 1.3** claims deterministic counting of perfect matchings in simple graphs in 2^{o(v+e)}, measured in vertices *plus edges*. It works through a count-preserving 3-CNF with one variable per edge ([OpenAI paper, pp.2–3](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). This is **claimed**. (Inference: for a sparse n×n 0/1 matrix it beats Ryser's roughly 2^n; for a dense one it gives 2^{o(n²)}, which is no improvement.) Boson-sampling amplitudes are permanents of dense complex submatrices of Haar-random unitaries, close to i.i.d. Gaussian ([arXiv:1011.3245](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3245)). A text search of OpenAI's paper found no "boson", "hafnian" or "quantum" ([OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). (Inference: the same sparse-only caveat applies to hafnians, which count perfect matchings in general graphs.)

**Per-int-NSETH(0.999)**, behind the 93- and 98-photon figures, concerns non-deterministic algorithms on dense integer matrices ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). (Inference: the encoding has about n² variables, so no contradiction follows.) The 98-photon figure is the 500-element one and also uses the linear-scaling assumption of section 4. (Inference: the claims give no simulator for linear-optical circuits with few elements, because their amplitudes carry complex weights that fall outside both labs' theorems as stated, section 6. Nothing in the claims contradicts that assumption for boson sampling.) The conjecture's unconditional support, Jerrum and Snir's monotone-circuit bound, is untouched. Its ETH-based support becomes vacuous ([arXiv:1805.05224](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.05224)). The concrete runtime claims for photonic experiments come from algorithms: exact boson sampling in O(n·2^n + poly(m,n)) ([Clifford and Clifford, arXiv:1706.01260](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.01260)), hafnians in O(n³·2^{n/2}) ([arXiv:1805.12498](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12498)), and loop-hafnian methods that reduced Jiuzhang's original 600-million-year estimate to 82 minutes for a single 92-photon event ([Bulmer et al., arXiv:2108.01622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.01622)). None of these algorithms uses SAT counting.

Commenters on the proof have noticed its permanent structure. Ryan O'Donnell is rewriting OpenAI's proof as a permanent calculation ([Links To Other Work](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/29/links-to-other-work/)). Shachar Lovett describes the approach as evaluating permanents of sparse Boolean matrices, and Nathan Lindzey asks which permanent properties it uses ([ethdiscussion.blog, 26 October post](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/)). Nobody has connected this to boson sampling (inference: sparse 0/1 permanents are a different object from Gaussian complex ones under approximation).

## 6. Partition functions and tensor networks

Internally, both algorithms contract signed-integer tensor networks. Anthropic's networks carry arbitrary integer tables, and the paper identifies its sums with weighted #CSPs, partition functions of pairwise Markov random fields "for nonnegative weights", and closed tensor networks. It claims no physics application ([Anthropic paper, pp.3, 14–15](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)), citing for those identifications [Dyer, Goldberg and Jerrum](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.3683) and [Robeva and Seigal](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.01437). OpenAI's Theorem D.1 has entries in a modular-integer ring ([OpenAI paper, p.53](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). Keyword searches of both papers found no claims about Ising, Potts or Tutte evaluation, complex weights or quantum circuits ([Anthropic paper](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf); [OpenAI paper](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf)). (Inference: rational, real and complex weights fall outside both theorems as stated.)

The established hardness results are all #ETH-conditional reductions from #3-SAT. Dell et al. prove exp(Ω(n))-type bounds for the Tutte polynomial, and hence the Potts model, at almost all hard rational points, noting that planar Ising lies on the hyperbola (x−1)(y−1) = 2 ([arXiv:1206.1775](https://arxiv.org/abs/1206.1775)). Curticapean extends this to sparse simple graphs and to the matching and independent-set polynomials ([arXiv:1511.02910](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.02910)). Liu gives a 2^{O(√(ΔN))} algorithm for planar Boolean tensor networks and a matching #ETH lower bound ([arXiv:2001.10204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.10204)). All of these lose their premise (rows C1–C3). Cai and Chen's complex #CSP dichotomy is a P versus #P-hard statement with no exponential-time content, and it is untouched (row C6) ([arXiv:1111.2384](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.2384)). So are Scott and Sorkin's Ising upper bounds ([arXiv:cs/0604079](https://arxiv.org/abs/cs/0604079)).

**The chromatic-polynomial point** is the one place where the labs claim an algorithm that contradicts a #ETH prediction directly. OpenAI's Corollary 1.3 and Anthropic's Corollary 7 claim 2^{o(v)} counting of proper q-colourings for fixed q, on graphs of any edge density ([OpenAI paper, p.2](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Exponential-Time-Hypothesis-is-False-October-25-2026/paper.pdf); [Anthropic paper, p.21](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). (Inference: through the standard chromatic–Tutte identity these counts are Tutte values at (1−q, 0), inside Dell et al.'s Theorem 1.4(ii) region, which corresponds to the zero-temperature antiferromagnetic Potts model.) This is **claimed**. Majdoddin separately claims that Anthropic's recursion with integer tables computes the Max-3-SAT spectrum in 2^{O(m/√log m)} ([arXiv:2610.41622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41622)).

The researchers extend this by inference, and none of the following is claimed in any source. Finite-temperature zero-field Ising and integer-q Potts partition functions on sparse graphs would plausibly become 2^{o(n)} (inference: the coefficients of the partition-function polynomial form a Max-2-SAT spectrum with an encoding linear in the number of edges). On dense graphs this gives 2^{o(n²)}, worse than Scott–Sorkin. Non-integer q, as in random-cluster points, gains nothing (inference: the connectivity weight q^{k(A)} has no known linear-size CNF encoding). Lattice models gain nothing either (inference: planar contraction is already 2^{O(√N)} and 3D is 2^{O(N^{2/3})} by treewidth, both better than 2^{O(N/√log N)}; any gain is confined to expander-like networks such as spin glasses on random graphs).

The novel tensor-network content, if correct, concerns models of computation. Austrin, Kaski and Kubjas prove an unconditional lower bound for algorithms that contract along a single contraction tree ([arXiv:1712.09630](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.09630)). Anthropic calculates that no exponent below about 2/9 is achievable in that model, and claims to escape it by writing the value as a signed sum of differently ordered contractions ([Anthropic paper, pp.3–4, 45–46](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/blob/16a5ce41304fcdaea4e0be7c132e1c361dc26da5/eth/paper.pdf)). Majdoddin formalises this as "Signed Network Sums" and claims an N^{23/6} contraction of the complete four-node network if ω = 2, below the N^4 floor for every contraction tree ([arXiv:2610.41622](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41622)). The AKK theorem itself stands (row C5), since it covers only its model. Dias and Lima find that OpenAI's interpolation costs 2^{Ω(√tw)} at treewidth tw ([arXiv:2611.00517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00517)). The researchers judge that these claims change fine-grained theory, not physics computation.

## 7. What has been said so far

Very little has been said. **Scott Aaronson's** post of 26 October reports the OpenAI claim but draws no physics link ([Shtetl-Optimized, ?p=10239](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)). In its 162-comment thread no comment mentions quantum supremacy, random-circuit sampling, BosonSampling, the HNS or DHKL papers, tensor networks or partition functions. His closest remark answers a hypothetical about AI-found classical algorithms in general: "None of this would put QM in jeopardy" ([?p=10239](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10239)). His statement "No, this doesn't kill quantum supremacy" appears in his 8 November post and concerns the separate Aaronson–Ambainis claim, not ETH ([?p=10295](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10295)). In that thread one reader asks whether the enormous sizes ETH needs defy physics, and nobody replies ([?p=10295](https://scottaaronson.blog/?p=10295)).

On **ethdiscussion.blog**, the only quantum-technical item is the Gheorghiu–Gupte comment described in section 3, and nobody has replied to it ([3 November](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/11/01/announce-your-results/)). The examiners stress that "Our involvement should not be taken as certification of the claim" ([examiners' letter](https://ethdiscussion.blog/2026/10/26/eth-refutation-examination-and-discussion/)). Lance Fortnow writes that the failure of ETH and SETH "doesn't mean their implications are false" ([Fortnow](https://blog.computationalcomplexity.org/2026/10/the-exponential-time-refutation.html)).

In the **press**, The Quantum Insider links arXiv:1804.10368 as a SETH-based simulation result whose broader conclusions would need another basis, says non-SETH findings would still hold, and quotes no experts ([TQI, 27 October](https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/10/27/openai-computing-claim-faces-review-raising-questions-about-quantum-advantage/)). Quantum Zeitgeist states without sources that random-circuit sampling hardness rests on non-collapse of the polynomial hierarchy, though conversions to qubit counts are "more exposed". It also says Shor's algorithm and black-box Grover optimality are unchanged ([Quantum Zeitgeist](https://quantumzeitgeist.substack.com/p/the-openai-exponential-time-hypothesis)). A *Science* article could not be read ([Science](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-cracks-27-year-old-pillar-computer-science-openai-and-anthropic-claim)).

On **arXiv**, abstract searches found no quant-ph or cond-mat.stat-mech paper citing the claims between 26 October and 11 November. Responses so far are in cs.CC and cs.DS ([arXiv API query](http://export.arxiv.org/api/query?search_query=abs:%22exponential%20time%20hypothesis%22&sortBy=submittedDate&sortOrder=descending&max_results=40)). The researchers' searches of the blogs, discussion threads and quantum press listed in this section found no public statement from Harrow, Fefferman, Hangleiter, Eisert or Preskill; social media was not searched. That absence is a finding, but it is not evidence of their views.

## 8. Open questions worth putting to experts

- Does the claimed algorithm's dependence on k reach CNF-SAT with poly(n) clauses and unbounded width, the literal hypothesis of HNS Theorem 3? OpenAI hides k in O_k, and Anthropic's stated exponent carries a k² factor ([companion report](p-vs-np-and-eth-claims.md)). Best placed: Huang, Newman and Szegedy; the examiners Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams.
- Do either lab's methods extend to Gaussian or cyclotomic integers, which would cover amplitudes with Hadamard and T phases and complex Potts points? Best placed: the labs' authors, Majdoddin, and O'Donnell, who is writing the proof up as a permanent calculation.
- Do the 500-element estimates (208/420/98) survive if the claims hold? They assume simulation cost linear in the number of circuit elements, while circuits with O(n) elements encode into CNF formulas with O(n) variables (section 4). Is there any reduction from the dense instances that would reach the conjectures themselves? Best placed: Dalzell, Harrow, Koh and La Placa.
- Is Morimae–Tamaki Conjecture 7 refuted by an exact #3-SAT counter, as the researchers infer from the definitions? Best placed: Morimae and Tamaki.
- What exactly do Gheorghiu and Gupte mean by "exact sampling", which gate set do they use, and do they handle T gates? Best placed: Gheorghiu and Gupte.
- Do fixed-width QSETH variants fall with the classical hypothesis, and if so which quantum lower bounds lose their premise? Best placed: Buhrman, Patro and Speelman; Chen and Chen; Deshpande and Mishra.
- Can signed sums of contractions beat contraction trees on expander-like physics networks at any feasible size? Best placed: Austrin, Kaski and Kubjas; Majdoddin.
- Which hypothesis does the Hangleiter–Eisert review intend for the HNS bound? Best placed: Hangleiter and Eisert.

## What could not be confirmed

The Gheorghiu–Gupte note, its definitions and its proofs were not read. The k-dependence needed to settle row A2 was not found in any source. The journal version of HNS (IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 2020) was not read ([dblp](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/tit/HuangNS20.html)), nor was the conference version of Aaronson–Chen. The Jiuzhang and Borealis papers were not fetched, so their exact hardness citations are unverified. DHKL's per-int-NSETH wording was read from a garbled extraction, and figures from its earlier versions were not checked. Liu's small-tensor-set result was taken from a search snippet. Searches covered arXiv abstracts only, and no social media was searched. The *Science* article returned an access error. Every inference marked above, including the refutation of Conjecture 7, the Tseitin blow-up, the consequence for the 500-element figures (added 12 November) and the Ising extension, is the researchers' own and unreviewed. The QAOA case of that consequence was not worked through. Neither lab's tensor-contraction theorem has been independently verified.
